# OME345 Applied Design Thinking - Lecture Notes

## 1. Introduction to Design Thinking
Design Thinking is a human-centered, iterative problem-solving methodology used to understand users, challenge assumptions, redefine problems, and create innovative solutions.

## 2. Five Stages of Design Thinking
1. Empathize: Understand user needs, motivations, and pain points through interviews, observation, and immersion.
2. Define: Formulate a clear problem statement (Point of View - POV) based on user insights gathered during empathy research.
3. Ideate: Generate a wide range of creative ideas using techniques like brainstorming, mind mapping, and Worst Possible Idea.
4. Prototype: Build low-fidelity, inexpensive physical or digital representations of concepts (paper wireframes, cardboard models).
5. Test: Gather user feedback on prototypes to refine understanding of user needs and iterate on solutions.

## 3. Key Mindsets in Design Thinking
- User-Centricity: Always keep the human end-user at the core of decision-making.
- Bias Toward Action: Focus on building and testing over endless discussion.
- Radical Collaboration: Leverage diverse team backgrounds for multifaceted perspectives.
- Embracing Failure: View prototypes that fail as cheap learning opportunities.
